How Split works, and how to use it well

Split turns one secret into several shares. You choose how many shares to make and how many are needed to get the secret back. That many shares together rebuild it; with fewer, every possible secret is equally likely, so they give away nothing.

  • Spread them out. Give each share to a different person, or keep them in different places: a safe, a relative, your lawyer, a bank box. Never keep enough shares together in one place.
  • Choose the numbers with care. Needing 2 of 3 is forgiving if one share is lost. Needing 3 of 5 or more makes it harder for people to combine shares without you. Make sure enough shares stay reachable even if one or two are lost.
  • Keep sets apart. All shares from one split carry the same set ID, and shares from different splits can’t be combined. A new split never updates old shares, so destroy the old ones when you replace a secret.
  • Every share is checked. A built-in check catches typing mistakes in each share, and the rebuilt secret is checked before it is shown, so a damaged or wrong share can’t quietly give you a wrong secret.
  • Nothing leaves your device. Shares are made in this browser tab. The secret is cleared from the page as soon as the shares are ready, and printing and saving happen on your own computer.

The method. Split uses Shamir’s Secret Sharing over the finite field GF(2⁸), the method published by Adi Shamir in 1979. Random numbers come from the browser’s crypto.getRandomValues. The secret is padded to a multiple of 16 bytes, so shares show only its approximate length, and it carries a 4-byte SHA-256 check. Each share ends with a CRC-32 typo check and is written in Crockford Base32.

Limits. Shares are only as safe as the places you keep them: anyone who collects enough of them can rebuild the secret. The QR code on each card holds the same code as the text, for scanning with a phone; this page can’t read QR codes itself, so type the code or open a saved share file. Keep an offline copy of this page, or note where to find it, so the shares stay usable.

Split works offline and can be saved and run from a USB stick like the other Encryption 1™ tools. Shares made on a page in one language can be rebuilt on a page in any other.
